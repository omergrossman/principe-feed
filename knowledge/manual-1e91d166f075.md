Sorry, Cyber: You Aren't the Only Ones Saving the Company from Itself

A security leader argues that cybersecurity teams are not uniquely positioned in organizations to counterbalance risky business decisions; rather, control functions across finance, operations, safety, legal, and compliance routinely face pressure to compromise on risk management. Drawing parallels to financial controls (mark-to-market accounting disciplines at Goldman Sachs pre-2008), the piece catalogs 20+ real-world scenarios where revenue, operations, or product teams pressure control functions to bypass safeguards—from recognizing premature revenue and falsifying expense classifications to deploying software with known data leakage. Effective risk governance depends on embedding controls into systems and processes (with escalation gates at CFO or equivalent level), supported by policies and executive backing. However, the author emphasizes that security and control teams must also reduce friction by identifying root causes of bypass requests—such as misaligned production cadences forcing emergency changes—rather than treating all pushback as obstruction.
