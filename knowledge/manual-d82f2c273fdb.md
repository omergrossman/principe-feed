Control Reliability Engineering: Applying SRE Principles to Cybersecurity Controls

Control Reliability Engineering (CRE) adapts Site Reliability Engineering principles to address the operational decay and misconfiguration of security controls that underpin most breach incidents. Rather than treating controls as one-time deployments, CRE applies continuous monitoring, Service Level Indicators/Objectives (SLIs/SLOs), error budgets, and blameless post-mortems to ensure controls remain functional and measurable. The framework shifts security from reactive ticket-based responses to metric-driven health monitoring, standardizes control deployment through code and CI/CD pipelines, and introduces synthetic event injection to detect failed controls before attackers exploit them. Ten essential elements include control cataloging, controls-as-code versioning, continuous control monitoring, control readiness reviews (analogous to production readiness reviews), safe staged deployments, and formalized control incident management with root-cause analysis.
