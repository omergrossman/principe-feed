Passwordless Authentication as Structural Enterprise Requirement in 2026

Passwordless authentication using FIDO2 and passkeys has transitioned from emerging practice to mandatory requirement for enterprise identity security, driven by phishing-resistance demands, regulatory pressure (NIST AM2/AM3, EIDAS), and zero-trust architecture evolution. The critical vulnerability in current deployments is insecure recovery and enrollment flows; attackers now target helpdesk processes rather than passwords directly, with recent breaches at MGM Resorts and Marks & Spencer exploiting weak recovery mechanisms. Effective governance requires four pillars: identity environment verification, device binding via hardware-backed storage (TPM, Secure Enclave), lifecycle management across managed and personal devices, and hardened recovery using verified device rebinding, identity proofing, or policy-controlled helpdesk flows with audit traces. Modern solutions integrate continuous authentication through device posture assessment, contextual risk engines evaluating geovelocity and IP reputation, and emerging Shared Signals Framework for cross-tool risk sharing. Implementation challenges center on legacy systems (Radius, VPN, VDI, mainframes) lacking FIDO2 protocol support; recommended approach prioritizes SaaS and managed endpoints first, applying equivalent-security alternatives for legacy gaps rather than password reversion.
