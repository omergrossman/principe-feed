Mastercard Cyber Pulse Report 2026 - EEMEA

Mastercard's regional threat assessment covering Eastern Europe, Middle East, and Africa (March 2025–March 2026) identifies a 13% year-on-year increase in cyber attacks, with activity shifting from high-volume campaigns toward targeted, multi-stage operations linked to geopolitical events. The region experiences breach costs 61% above the global average ($7.29M in the Middle East versus $4.44M globally), while cyber activity spikes dramatically during geopolitical crises—a 198% increase occurred on the first day of recent Middle East conflict, establishing a critical 48–72 hour response window. Critical infrastructure, financial services, government, technology, and healthcare sectors face sustained targeting by state-sponsored actors, organized crime, and hacktivist groups using malware, ransomware, and email-based social engineering. Despite 10% year-on-year security spending growth, organizations show persistent gaps in application security, encryption, patching discipline, and governance; talent shortages (300,000+ unfilled roles across MENA, 20,000 certified professionals across Africa) constrain defensive capabilities. Ukraine, Pakistan, and UAE face highest attack volumes, with the Middle East representing 49% of regional attacks.
