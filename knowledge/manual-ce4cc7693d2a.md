Why Post-Quantum Cryptography Starts With Credentials

Organizations face a dual timeline threat from quantum computing: cryptographically relevant quantum computers may emerge within 15 years according to the Global Risk Institute's 2025 survey, yet adversaries can exploit Harvest Now, Decrypt Later tactics by capturing encrypted credentials today for future decryption. The NSA's Commercial National Security Algorithm Suite 2.0 mandates quantum-resistant support by January 1, 2027, with full transition required by 2035; NIST's draft IR 8547 similarly deprecates RSA-2048 and ECC P-256 after 2030. Credentials—particularly long-lived Non-Human Identities (service accounts and API keys)—present disproportionate risk due to extended confidentiality lifetimes and poor inventory coverage. A credentials-first migration strategy should prioritize cryptographic inventory, risk-based prioritization by exposure and lifetime rather than dataset size, hybrid cryptography combining classical and quantum-resistant algorithms, and crypto-agility through centralized key management to enable future algorithm updates without re-engineering.
