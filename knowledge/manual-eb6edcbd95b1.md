Technology Waves and Security - Is This Time Really Different?

Security architecture has historically lagged during technology transformations, with the pattern repeating across mainframe-to-PC, Internet adoption, mobile, and cloud migrations. While early waves often lacked foundational security (TCP/IP protocols, early Windows), subsequent phases improved incrementally—mobile platforms benefited from Apple and Google's efforts, and cloud providers introduced encryption and partitioning by default with shared responsibility models. Security design patterns emerge only after initial market adoption and experimentation clarify how interconnected system components should be arranged; the DMZ pattern, for instance, crystallized after years of Internet infrastructure evolution, enabling proper placement of identity, access control, and filtering mechanisms. AI represents a more complex challenge, playing out simultaneously across consumer/business, edge/core, and software/business processes, requiring deterministic safeguards on non-deterministic technology. The author observes that security improvements typically come through standards development, ecosystem innovation, and sustained startup competition rather than preemptive design, and that this pattern is repeating with AI despite greater collective awareness.
