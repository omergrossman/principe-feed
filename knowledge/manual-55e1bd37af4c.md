Detection engineering: A programmatic approach to identifying cyber threats

Detection engineering has evolved from a niche practice into a core security discipline, with 80% of organizations and 85% of large enterprises now actively investing in dedicated detection engineering teams according to a SANS Institute and Anvilogic survey of 264 cybersecurity professionals. Unlike traditional threat detection that relies on static vendor-provided rules and signatures, detection engineering applies software development principles to create customized, behavior-driven detection logic tailored to an organization's specific environment and threat landscape. Key adoption drivers include high false positive rates (64% of surveyed organizations), poor environmental accuracy in generic detections (61%), and the inadequacy of signature-based approaches against advanced threats such as fileless malware, living-off-the-land techniques, zero-day exploits, and supply chain attacks. Implementation requires integration of threat intelligence, threat modeling, and SDLC/CI/CD practices, along with centralized data aggregation from endpoints, networks, cloud environments, and security tools to reduce alert fatigue and dwell time.
