Threat actors are recruiting the people who hold cloud logins

Intel 471 research documents a structured approach by threat actors to compromise cloud environments through insider recruitment and credential harvesting. The analysis categorizes insider risk into three types: negligent insiders (who create the most risk through poor hygiene like password reuse and disabled MFA), manipulated insiders (targeted via social engineering and phishing kits), and malicious insiders (willingly selling access). Information-stealer malware including Vidar, Stealc_v2, and ACR actively harvest credentials and session cookies for services such as Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and Slack, which are then sold to initial access brokers. Adversary-in-the-middle toolkits and phishing kits targeting platforms like Okta and Gmail are commoditized in underground markets, with documented examples including an actor claiming successful bribery of an Indian company employee for Salesforce access containing 2.3 million customer records. Recommended mitigations include enforcing least-privilege access reviews, centralizing third-party app inventories, deploying phishing-resistant MFA, and implementing SaaS usage monitoring.
